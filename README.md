## Introduction

Applied ethics is the practice of analyzing concrete moral problems using the tools of philosophical reasoning. While normative ethics examines what makes actions right or lives good in the abstract, applied ethics tests those principles against difficult real-world dilemmas: medical triage, automated surveillance, corporate whistleblowing, and algorithmic bias. The goal of studying applied ethics is not to adopt a fixed set of answers, but to build a structured way to evaluate conflicting moral claims, identify underlying assumptions, and defend judgments with coherent arguments.

This curriculum assumes no prior background in philosophy, formal logic, or professional ethics. It is designed for self-directed learners who want a rigorous, grounded foundation in ethical reasoning. All the open textbooks, lecture series, and primary resources selected here are accessible to general readers without requiring institutional access or instructor guidance.

### How the Curriculum Is Organized

The curriculum is structured in two stages: foundational tools, followed by specific applied domains.

The first three subjects provide the shared toolkit needed to analyze moral problems:

- Foundations of Ethical Theory introduces the major normative frameworks — utilitarianism, Kantian deontology, virtue ethics, and contractualism—alongside basic questions about moral objectivity.
- Logic and Critical Reasoning develops the analytical skills required to identify, reconstruct, and evaluate arguments.
- Methods and Frameworks in Applied Ethics teaches practical techniques for bridging theory and practice, including principlism, casuistry, reflective equilibrium, and structured decision models.

Because these three subjects establish the concepts and analytical methods that the rest of the curriculum relies on, you should work through them in order before moving forward.

Once the foundations are in place, the remaining three subjects—Bioethics and Medical Ethics, Business and Professional Ethics, and Ethics of Technology and AI — can be studied in any order based on your background or interests. Each domin examines how ethical principles operate within specific institutional, commercial, and technological contexts.

### Scope

This curriculum focuses on the essential core of the discipline: the fundamental normative theories, argument skills, decision frameworks, and three canonical applied domains. It does not attempt to survey every specialized subfield, such as environmental ethics, military ethics, or legal philosophy.

Once you have completed these foundational subjects, you can explore specialized fields in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md) or practice evaluating scenarios using [Projects](projects.md).

## Curriculum

### Foundations of Ethical Theory

The major normative theories — utilitarianism, Kantian deontology, virtue ethics, and contractualism — plus the basic metaethical questions about what moral claims are and whether they can be objective.

[Introduction to Ethics: Moral Problems and the Good Life (MITx / MIT Open Learning Library)](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+24.02x+2T2020/about) - free interactive course requiring no registration; covers well-being, moral objectivity, historical approaches, and what we owe to others.

[Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature (Open Yale Courses / Tamar Gendler)](https://oyc.yale.edu/philosophy/phil-181) - complete free video lectures pairing classic texts from Plato, Aristotle, Kant, Mill, and Rawls with findings from cognitive science.

[Justice with Michael Sandel (Harvard University / YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL30C13C91CFFEFEA6) - Harvard's famous introductory series that stress-tests utilitarian, Kantian, and libertarian theories against real moral dilemmas.

[Ethics for A-Level (Mark Dimmock & Andrew Fisher / Open Book Publishers)](https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0125) - free open-access textbook covering normative ethics, metaethics, and applied topics in a rigorous but readable way.

[The Elements of Moral Philosophy (James Rachels & Stuart Rachels / McGraw-Hill)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781259914256) - the most widely used concise ethics textbook; a good paid alternative spine if you want a traditional single-author survey.

### Logic and Critical Reasoning

How to identify, reconstruct, and evaluate arguments — the core skill that all ethical analysis and case discussion depends on.

[Introduction to Logic and Critical Thinking (Matthew Van Cleave / Open Textbook Library)](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/introduction-to-logic-and-critical-thinking) - free open textbook covering argument identification, reconstruction, informal fallacies, and basic formal logic.

[forall x: Calgary (Open Logic Project)](https://forallx.openlogicproject.org) - free open formal logic textbook for learners who want symbolic depth beyond informal argument analysis.

### Methods and Frameworks in Applied Ethics

How to move from theory to practice — case analysis, principlism, casuistry, and reflective equilibrium, plus structured decision-making frameworks for real dilemmas.

[A Framework for Ethical Decision Making (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics, Santa Clara University)](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making) - free guide that walks through recognizing issues, gathering facts, and evaluating alternatives through utilitarian, rights, fairness, common-good, and virtue lenses.

[NHSEB Case Library (National High School Ethics Bowl)](https://nhseb.org/case-library) - free, searchable archive of over 300 real-world ethics cases with discussion questions; the practice ground for applying the frameworks.

[Practical Ethics (Peter Singer / Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521707688) - the canonical demonstration of systematic reasoning from theory to concrete problems such as equality, animals, and climate.

[Principles of Biomedical Ethics (Tom L. Beauchamp & James F. Childress / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780190640873) - the definitive statement of principlism and of the specification and balancing methods used across applied ethics.

### Bioethics and Medical Ethics

The most developed applied domain — consent, autonomy, end-of-life care, reproduction, research ethics, and allocation — taught through canonical cases and the four-principles approach.

[Bioethics: The Law, Medicine, and Ethics of Reproductive Technologies and Genetics (Harvard Professional and Lifelong Learning)](https://pll.harvard.edu/course/bioethics-law-medicine-and-ethics-reproductive-technologies-and-genetics) - free self-paced course on the edX platform covering surrogacy, enhancement, genetic information, and tissue ownership.

[Bioethics: A Coursebook (Compost Collective / Open Book Publishers)](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0449/) - free open-access coursebook with chapters on health care ethics, research ethics, and emerging biotechnology.

[Medical Ethics Online (Peoples-uni / NextGenU)](https://courses.nextgenu.org/course/view.php?id=337) - free self-directed course on consent, confidentiality, and end-of-life decisions, built for independent learners.

### Business and Professional Ethics

Ethical reasoning applied to markets, firms, and professional roles — corporate responsibility, stakeholder theory, whistleblowing, and the behavioral science of why good people do bad things.

[Business Ethics (Stephen M. Byars & Kurt Stanberry / OpenStax)](https://openstax.org/details/books/business-ethics) - free peer-reviewed textbook covering ethical theory, stakeholders, corporate social responsibility, and workplace cases.

[Ethics Unwrapped (McCombs School of Business, UT Austin)](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu) - free short-video series explaining behavioral ethics concepts like bounded ethicality, incrementalism, and moral disengagement.

[Giving Voice to Values (Mary C. Gentile)](https://givingvoicetovaluesthebook.com) - free curriculum focused on the practical side — scripts, rehearsal, and pre-scripting for acting on your values under organizational pressure.

### Ethics of Technology and AI

The ethical analysis of computing, data, and algorithmic systems — privacy, surveillance, bias, accountability, and the responsible design of AI.

[Ethics of AI (University of Helsinki)](https://ethics-of-ai.mooc.fi) - free interactive course covering AI ethics concepts through short lessons and practical exercises.

[Global MOOC on the Ethics of AI (UNESCO / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/global-mooc-on-the-ethics-of-ai) - free 10-module course built on UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI; audit access on Coursera.

[Weapons of Math Destruction (Cathy O'Neil / Broadway Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780553418835) - accessible case studies of how algorithms in policing, insurance, education, and credit can reinforce inequality.
